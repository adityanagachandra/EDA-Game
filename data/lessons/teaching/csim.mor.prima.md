# PRIMA and passive reduction

PRIMA reduces large linear RLC networks by projecting their MNA matrices onto a Krylov subspace. It matches moments like AWE, but implicitly and stably, through an orthonormal basis built by block Arnoldi, and its congruence projection preserves passivity. Post-layout parasitic networks with millions of elements are routinely reduced this way before simulation or timing analysis.

## Moments matched by a block Krylov basis

For an interconnect with $p$ ports, the moments of the port admittance are generated by the Krylov sequence $R, AR, A^2R, \ldots$ with $A = -G^{-1}C$ and $R = G^{-1}B$, where $B$ selects the ports. Block Arnoldi builds an orthonormal basis $V$ of this space, $p$ columns per iteration. A basis of $q$ columns, $q = mp$, matches the first $m = q/p$ block moments. An 8-port net reduced with $q = 48$ columns matches 6 block moments. Orthonormalization is what makes this stable where AWE fails: the basis vectors stay well separated instead of all aligning with the dominant eigenvector.

## Congruence projection

PRIMA projects the MNA matrices by congruence: $\hat{G} = V^T G V$, $\hat{C} = V^T C V$, $\hat{B} = V^T B$. For a two-node conductance matrix $G = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$ mS and a single basis vector $v = (0.6, 0.8)$, $\hat{G} = 3 \cdot 0.36 - 2 \cdot 0.48 + 2 \cdot 0.64 = 1.4$ mS. When $G$ and $C$ are arranged so that their symmetric parts are positive semidefinite, which MNA allows for RLC networks, congruence preserves that property, and the reduced model is passive: it cannot generate energy, so connecting it to other passive circuits cannot create instability. Moment matching alone, as in AWE, gives no such guarantee.

## The reduced pole

A one-column reduction of an RC network has one state and one pole, $-\hat{G}/\hat{C}$. With $G$ as above and $C = \mathrm{diag}(1, 0.5)$ pF, $\hat{C} = 0.36 + 0.32 = 0.68$ pF and the pole is $-1.4/0.68 = -2.06$ 1/ns (millisiemens per picofarad is one per nanosecond). Because $\hat{G}$ and $\hat{C}$ are positive, the pole is always in the left half plane. In practice $q$ is chosen to cover the frequency band of interest, the reduced model is a small dense block stamped into MNA, and its order trades accuracy against the cost of that dense block.
